02 · Evidence catalogue

Recover the space

Absence from the surviving dialogue does not imply absence from the dramatic environment. However, presence in the material world does not establish authorial intention, nor knowledge or understanding by any particular receiver.

Explicit evidence is distinctly referenced within Plato’s text. Contextual evidence is derived from the setting or scene that is referenced by the text.

05 · Social memory

Considering the
Collective Memory

The claim is not that “Athenians thought X”, but that some contemporary receivers may have possessed contextual resources, associations and narratives that modern readers usually lack.

01
People and reputation

Socrates and Euthyphro

The encounter begins with two culturally legible figures rather than anonymous speakers. Socrates could already carry associations from comedy and public reputation; Euthyphro presents himself as unusually knowledgeable about divine matters. The question is not what every receiver thought, but what prior narratives may have been available when these names appeared together.

02
Place and institution

The Stoa Basileios

The Stoa is explicitly named. Its legal, political and religious associations form part of the recoverable context of the scene. The research question is what the place-name may have activated for a receiver who knew the institution and its setting.

03
Material culture and intertextuality

The Herms

The herm bases are not named in the dialogue, but archaeology and ancient testimony place a dense hermic presence in this part of the Agora. Their wider social narrative includes Thucydides’ account of the mutilation of the herms and the political anxiety surrounding it. This creates a testable relationship between material environment, remembered event and reception.

04
Proximity and possible association

Zeus Eleutherios

The Stoa of Zeus Eleutherios stood close to the encounter zone. Traditions that connect Socrates with discussions there make his presence at the Basileios potentially notable, while Euthyphro later invokes Zeus as a precedent in his defence of prosecuting his father. The proximity is therefore something to test, not evidence of Plato’s intention.

06 · Reading

What becomes visible
when context is recovered?

This final page demonstrates the value of the method: relationships that are absent from the surviving wording can nevertheless become available for scholarly investigation when text, material evidence, space and collective memory are analysed together.

01 · Surviving text

Stoa Basileios

The dialogue explicitly places Socrates at the Basileus’ stoa. The text does not enumerate the surrounding material environment.

02 · Recovered space

Herms enter the scene

Archaeology and ancient testimony recover a substantial hermic presence around this part of the northwest Agora.

03 · Social narrative

Material becomes memory

Thucydides’ account of the mutilation of the herms connects these objects with sacrilege, political anxiety and remembered civic crisis.

04 · New question

A hidden relationship is exposed

Does the material and remembered landscape alter the contextual horizon within which Socrates and Euthyphro discuss piety, prosecution and the gods?

Example 01 · Herms

Material culture + memory

Text-only: the herms are invisible. Recovered context: they become materially present and historically charged. The project does not claim Plato intended a fixed hermic meaning; it makes the relationship available to test.

Example 02 · Stoa Basileios

A place-name becomes an institution

Text-only: a named location. Recovered context: legal, political and religious associations around the Archon Basileus become part of the analytical field.

Example 03 · Zeus Eleutherios

Proximity generates a question

Text-only: Zeus appears later in Euthyphro’s argument. Recovered context: the neighbouring Stoa of Zeus Eleutherios creates a spatial relationship worth testing alongside Socratic habit and divine precedent.

What the proof of concept demonstrates

Decontextualisation can remove analytical data

A conventional text-only reading may never reveal some relationships embedded in the dramatic environment. Recovering spatial, archaeological and social-memory evidence does not recover authorial intention or a single ancient reading. It recovers contextual relationships, patterns and possibilities that can then be tested.

From one opening scene to a research programme

What happens when the same method is applied systematically across Plato?

This proof of concept uses only Euthyphro 2a–5a. Applied across the corpus, the method can be used to identify and compare:

  • recurring spatial patterns and clusters;
  • concentrations of material and religious evidence;
  • relationships between places, people, institutions and concepts;
  • cross-dialogue associations and repeated locations;
  • possible social-memory connections that a text-only approach may not expose;
  • patterns large enough to be queried, mapped and compared rather than noticed only incidentally.
