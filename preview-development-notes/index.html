02 · Evidence catalogue

Recover the space
in and around the text

Absence from the surviving dialogue does not imply absence from the dramatic environment. However, presence in the material world does not establish authorial intention, nor knowledge or understanding by any particular receiver.

EvidenceExplicit evidence is distinctly referenced within Plato’s text. Contextual evidence is derived from the setting or scene that is referenced by the text.

dramatic landscape

Use location, evidence type and proximity to ask whether patterns emerge between what the dialogue names and what can be recovered from its setting.

Prototype pattern signal

A concentration of religious material and associations

Even across this deliberately small sample, religious or cult-related context recurs around the dramatic setting: the Stoa Basileios, herms, Zeus Eleutherios, shrine/votive material and the King Archon’s legal-religious role. This is not a statistical conclusion; it is a pattern worth testing against a larger dataset.

05 · Social memory

Considering the
Collective Memory

The claim is not that “Athenians thought X”, but that some contemporary receivers may have possessed contextual resources, associations and narratives that modern readers usually lack.

01

Socrates and Euthyphro as culturally loaded figures

Before the scene is interpreted, the names themselves may carry narrative baggage. A research reading can compare the comic Socrates of Aristophanes’ Clouds, the Socrates later presented at trial in Plato’s Apology, and the assumptions a receiver may bring to Euthyphro’s claims to religious expertise.

02

The Stoa Basileios

The setting is explicitly named. What political, legal and religious associations attach to the office and place of the Basileus, and where else are those associations recoverable in literary, epigraphic or archaeological evidence?

Research trail →
03

The Herm bases and wider social narrative

The herms are not enumerated in the dialogue, but their material presence and wider Athenian narrative invite intertextual comparison, including Thucydides’ account of the mutilation of the herms and its civic consequences.

Research trail →
04

Zeus Eleutherios and deviation in place

A potential association to test is the proximity of the Stoa of Zeus Eleutherios to the encounter zone. If traditions of Socratic discussion there are relevant, does Socrates’ presence at the Basileios represent a meaningful deviation? Does the later invocation of Zeus in Euthyphro’s argument make that spatial proximity worth testing, without treating it as authorial intention?
