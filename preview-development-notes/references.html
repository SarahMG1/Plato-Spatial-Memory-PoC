Purpose of this page

These entries provide the research trail behind the Evidence Catalogue. They are prompts for source checking and further reading, not a claim that every cited source supports every interpretative proposition.

Stoa Basileios

Royal Stoa / Stoa Basileios

  • Plato, Euthyphro 2a, in John Burnet (ed.), Platonis Opera, Vol. I (1900).
  • American School of Classical Studies at Athens, Athenian Agora excavation records and published discussions of the Royal Stoa.
  • The Athenian Agora III: Literary and Epigraphical Testimonia, for ancient testimony concerning the Stoa Basileios and the northwest Agora.
  • Jessica Paga (2021), Building Democracy in Late Archaic Athens, especially the Agora discussion for siting, movement and political landscape.
Herm bases / The Herms

Material presence and social narrative

  • American School of Classical Studies at Athens, excavation reports and records for herm bases around the Royal Stoa / northwest Agora.
  • The Athenian Agora III: Literary and Epigraphical Testimonia, testimony concerning the area known as “The Herms”.
  • Thucydides 6.27–29 for the mutilation of the herms and its civic and political consequences.
  • Brenda Steinbock (2012), Social Memory in Athenian Public Discourse, for broader methodological work on memory and public narrative.
Stoa of Zeus Eleutherios

Neighbouring civic and religious space

  • American School of Classical Studies at Athens, Agora plans and monument records for the Stoa of Zeus Eleutherios.
  • The Athenian Agora III, literary testimonia relating to the Stoa of Zeus and adjacent west/northwest Agora.
  • Jessica Paga (2021), Building Democracy in Late Archaic Athens, for spatial and ideological context.
  • Any claim concerning Socratic conversational practice at or around the Stoa of Zeus should be verified against the relevant ancient testimonia before being elevated from research prompt to stated evidence.
Panathenaic approach

Movement into the northwest Agora

  • American School of Classical Studies at Athens, published Agora plans showing the Panathenaic Way and northwest entrance.
  • Jessica Paga (2021), for movement, access, visibility and the Agora’s built environment.
  • ASCSA excavation reports for the relationship between the Royal Stoa, major roads and the northwest corner of the market square.
Shrine and votive material

Religious material east of the Royal Stoa

  • American School of Classical Studies at Athens excavation reports for the small Classical shrine and associated votive material near the Royal Stoa.
  • Relevant Agora excavation notebooks, plans, finds records and Hesperia reports should be used for chronology and find context before making claims about presence in 399 BCE.
Eridanos channel

Infrastructure and environmental context

  • American School of Classical Studies at Athens / Hesperia reports on the Eridanos and its Classical channel through and beside the Agora.
  • Use archaeological reconstruction to establish course and relationship; visual, acoustic or olfactory salience remains a separate and more uncertain question.
King Archon

Legal-religious institutional context

  • The Athenian Agora III: Literary and Epigraphical Testimonia, for the office of the Archon Basileus and the Royal Stoa.
  • American School of Classical Studies at Athens material on the Royal Stoa’s administrative and legal functions.
  • Plato, Euthyphro 2a–5a for the explicit dramatic and procedural setting.
Situated opening

Interpretative synthesis

  • Sarah Burke (2019), Plato’s Euthyphro and Phaedrus Dialogues: Inconsistent Gods, MA dissertation, The Open University — research origin for the present project.
  • John Burnet (ed.) (1900), Platonis Opera, Vol. I — canonical Greek textual base used in the prototype.
  • Brenda Steinbock (2012), for social-memory methodology.
  • Spatial-humanities precedents including HESTIA and GIS-based work on ancient texts provide methodological comparison rather than direct evidence for the Euthyphro interpretation.
Collective memory prompt

Socrates as a culturally loaded figure

  • Aristophanes, Clouds, for a comic public representation of Socrates prior to 399 BCE.
  • Plato, Apology, for Plato’s later dramatic presentation of Socrates at trial.
  • These texts should not be collapsed into a single “Athenian view” of Socrates; the point is to test what competing cultural representations could contribute to reception.
