02 · Evidence catalogue

Recover the world
around the text.

This catalogue now contains the contextual material previously shown as “What is implicit?”. It distinguishes explicit textual information from archaeological, topographical, institutional and interpretative evidence rather than inserting that evidence into Plato’s wording.

Methodological ruleAbsence from the surviving dialogue does not imply absence from the dramatic environment. Presence in the environment does not establish salience to every receiver.
Explicit · attested place

Stoa Basileios

Plato places Socrates at the Basileus’ stoa. Archaeology independently locates the Royal Stoa at the northwest corner of the Agora. Its precise rendered appearance in 399 BCE remains reconstructed.

Herm bases and “The Herms”

Institutional context

King Archon and legal-religious procedure

A receiver familiar with Athenian civic institutions may not have required the dialogue to explain why the Basileus’ stoa was an intelligible setting for a prosecution with religious dimensions.

Situated opening

Across 2a–5a the opening links piety, prosecution, office, place and material environment before sustained definitional inquiry begins. The significance of that conjunction remains an argument to be tested rather than an established fact.
